# BRIDGE — Business & Safety Officer Position on the Six Owner Questions

*Read against: `docs/memory/design/chain/00-overview.md`, `03-tokenomics-grow.md`, `04-onchain-recording-snapshots.md`, `05-custody-security.md`, `01-asset-lifecycle-contracts.md`; auto-feed leak verified in code at `src/growpodempire/simulation/engine.py:164-170`.*

## Q1 — Snapshot pricing after the free first one

**RULING: 25 GROW base / 75 GROW premium, adopted as proposed; first-EVER free only (not first-per-plant); price set in `balance.yaml` and communicated as a posted, stable "studio fee" with a 7-day notice on any change.**

**RATIONALE:** The locked debate ruling binds us: snapshots are priced ≥ real chain cost (~0.002 ALGO fees + 0.1 ALGO MBR locked when delivered as NFT). At any plausible GROW:ALGO rate, 25 GROW clears that with margin — and must keep clearing it. Commercially, 25 GROW ≈ a common seed is correct impulse pricing: SAGE projects −10 to −25 GROW/day per engaged player, making this the only daily-cadence, every-archetype sink in the drain plan. First-ever free is the only fiscally responsible free tier: first-per-plant turns a bounded one-time marketing cost into an unbounded leak that scales with exactly the whale behavior we are trying to drain; the free first snapshot being custodial-only keeps even that bounded cost off the MBR books.

**CONDITIONS:** (1) Standing peg check: if `25 GROW < 1.5 × (0.103 ALGO at current rate)` for 7 consecutive days, the economy report goes amber and the price is re-tuned — never sell below 1.5× real cost. (2) Price only in `balance.yaml` (`snapshots.price`, `snapshots.premium_price`). (3) Fixed menu price; any increase gets 7 days' notice. (4) One snapshot NFT per player per day stays the hard cap — it is also our treasury cost cap.

## Q2 — Whale-inflation control

**RULING: Both instruments, shaped differently — pod upkeep as a flat per-harvest percentage on everyone (3/5/8% by tier), and the NPC sale tax as a progressive threshold tax: 0% on the first 300 GROW of NPC sales per UTC day, 5% from 300–1,000, 8% above 1,000. Target: net issuance ≤ +20 GROW/day per active player at the mean AND p99 ≤ +50/day.**

**RATIONALE:** SAGE's audit is the controlling fact: the economy flips permanently inflationary at ~2 months; "inflation compounds with investment" because faucets scale with pod count while sinks don't. Pod upkeep is the structurally correct instrument because it is *proportional to the faucet itself* — it cannot be outgrown, and it directly counter-scales the pro-pod free-feed leak. Per-harvest percentage, not per-day rent: rent punishes slow careful growing, which is the sim's soul and the retention product. The threshold tax extracts almost the entire intended drain from precisely the top decile generating the inflation, while a flat 5% on everyone would shave the new-player floor for negligible gain. SAGE's sizing math shows snapshot + upkeep + tax brings the +75/day player to ~+22/day.

**CONDITIONS:** (1) Hard targets in the economy report: mean ≤ +20/day, **p99 ≤ +50/day** — track p90/p99, because whale inflation is the named failure mode and means hide it. (2) Faucet:sink ratio alarm at >1.5 for 7 days stays. (3) All knobs in `balance.yaml`. (4) Ship the recurring economy report *before or with* these sinks — taxing blind is how you over-drain and churn the mid-game. (5) The 50/day stipend is untouchable — it is the retention floor.

## Q3 — Pro-pod free auto-feed

**RULING: Close the leak. Auto-feed posts a discounted `NUTRIENT_PURCHASE` ledger charge (60% of manual price) from the service-layer catch-up path. The pro pod's perk becomes *convenience plus a discount*, not *exemption from the economy*.**

**RATIONALE:** Verified: `engine.py:167-170` refills nutrients with no ledger post — correct per the "no economy in the pure engine" invariant, but a one-time 1,200 GROW purchase **permanently deletes a recurring sink** for exactly the players whose faucets scale fastest. That is the worst possible shape: a one-time payment buying perpetual exemption from the drain. The fix belongs in `services/` so the pure engine stays pure. A discount preserves the pro pod's value proposition honestly while keeping every fed plant on the books. This also restores the invariant that **every spend posts to the ledger** — right now pro-pod nutrient consumption is invisible to the economy report, so our own inflation metrics undercount the leak.

**CONDITIONS:** (1) Discount percentage is a `balance.yaml` key. (2) Grandfather nothing — apply at the same patch as the upkeep sink, announced together as one "facility costs" change; two separate nerfs two weeks apart reads as farming. (3) If churn analytics show pro-pod owners leaving, the lever is the discount knob, not reverting the ledger post. (4) Insufficient balance: auto-feed skips (plant stress follows naturally) rather than driving balances negative — no involuntary debt, ever.

## Q4 — Tokenize-at-seed vs tokenize-anytime

**RULING: Tokenize-at-seed only at launch (Phases 0–2). Revisit tokenize-anytime no earlier than Phase 3, and only with a permanent provenance flag distinguishing the two classes.**

**RATIONALE:** Provenance integrity is the product — one asset following one organism with its full attested history is the Proof-of-Cultivation moat. A mid-life tokenization mints an asset whose pre-mint history is server-asserted but never chain-anchored, creating a two-tier provenance market. The exploit surface is strictly larger: tokenize-anytime lets a player *observe* a plant's trajectory and selectively tokenize only winners — adverse selection that interacts dangerously with the value floor, since ATLAS's money-pump invariant (`floor(any state) < seed cost + mint fee`) is calibrated on at-seed economics where the minter bears the full grow risk. Tokenizing a healthy flowering plant near harvest bears almost none of it. The standalone harvest-NFT path already covers the "I wish I'd tokenized this" regret case at the terminal state. You can always loosen provenance rules; you can never retroactively tighten assets already minted.

**CONDITIONS:** (1) Keep the standalone harvest-NFT path alive as the escape valve. (2) If tokenize-anytime ships later: immutable `tokenized_at_stage` metadata, and the floor schedule for mid-tokenized assets re-derived against the money-pump invariant per stage, covered by the same property test. (3) Mint fee charges at tokenization regardless — the fee is also the spam throttle on treasury-paid mint costs.

## Q5 — Snapshot NFT custody

**RULING: Confirm the ruling as written, with one amendment: a treasury MBR-lockup ceiling and a reclaim policy must ship with the product, not after it.**

**RATIONALE:** Custodial-by-default is the only shape serving pre-wallet-login players (the majority); the free first snapshot staying custodial-only caps marketing cost at fees rather than fees+0.1 ALGO; claim-to-wallet with the player paying their own opt-in MBR is the honest cost allocation. The claim transfer being "withdrawal-shaped" keeps it inside the one signing story with no new key exposure. The amendment targets ORACLE's flagged number: **10,000 custodial snapshot NFTs lock ~1,000 ALGO of treasury MBR** — fine as a paid product whose fee covers it, but only if the fee *actually* covers it forward and the lockup is monitored. At 100k snapshots it's ~10,000 ALGO locked — real balance-sheet exposure for a vanity product.

**CONDITIONS:** (1) Add `treasury_mbr_locked` to the economy report with an amber threshold at 20% of treasury ALGO holdings. (2) Price review triggers if cumulative snapshot revenue falls below cumulative MBR locked + fees paid — the product must be self-funding on a running basis. (3) When a player claims to wallet, the treasury's 0.1 MBR for that asset is *released* — make MBR-recovery explicit in the claim flow; claims actively de-risk the balance sheet, so make claiming easy, not grudging. (4) The claim path is a Phase 2 deliverable, not a someday — custodial-forever invites the regulatory question of whether we are holding players' property; a live, documented claim path is the answer.

## Q6 — Burn ceremonies

**RULING: No burn ceremonies at the env-var rung or the KMS rung. Permissible only at the multisig rung (MainNet gate) or later — and even then, fold the burn into the existing reconciler/anchor signing cadence rather than staging a separate "ceremony."**

**RATIONALE:** The optics benefit is marginal: with a fixed-supply ASA, in-game sink burns need *no* chain action because unbridged GROW never left the treasury — the deflation is already real and, post-reconciler, already **publicly auditable by anyone with an indexer**. A burn ceremony is theater duplicating a proof we already publish. The cost side is concrete: today the treasury is a single env-var mnemonic that is simultaneously ASA creator, manager, reserve, minter, and withdrawal signer. Every additional manually-initiated, human-staged treasury operation is another occasion to handle the key, another script with sign authority, another procedure an attacker can study or spoof. WARDEN's threat model says to assume the attacker reads this doc — a publicly pre-announced, calendar-scheduled treasury-key operation is a gift to that attacker. Soft regulatory point: ceremonial burns are the marketing grammar of speculative token projects; a game whose public posture is "the DB is truth, the chain mirrors" should not borrow that grammar.

**CONDITIONS:** (1) Hard gate: no burn before 2-of-3 multisig holds the GROW ASA manager/reserve roles AND the hot/cold split is live. (2) When permitted: burns go to the frozen burn address, are ledgered like every treasury move, execute at most quarterly, move only realized sink surplus (`Σ sinks − Σ faucets`, never principal), and ride an *already-scheduled* multisig session — no dedicated key-handling event. (3) Until then, the honest substitute ships now and costs nothing: publish the net-issuance and sink-surplus figures from the economy report as the public deflation story. Provable restraint beats performative fire.

## Closing — the single biggest risk and how these rulings contain it

The biggest financial risk among the six is not any one leak — it is the **compounding whale-inflation curve (Q2/Q3) hitting an economy that acquires a real off-ramp**. Today +200–300 GROW/day per whale is a balance problem; the moment the GROW ASA trades on MainNet with a withdrawal bridge, it becomes a treasury-solvency problem: every uncontained unit of net issuance is a future claim on the fixed-supply treasury via the bridge, and the issuance is permanent, compounding, and concentrated in the players most motivated to extract. The containment is deliberately layered and *measured before it is trusted*: Q3 closes the one sink whales could permanently delete; Q2 installs the two sinks that scale with the faucet itself, bound to hard numeric targets enforced by a recurring economy report that must ship first; Q1 adds the only every-player daily drain at a price pegged above real chain cost; Q4 and Q5 prevent the asset layer from opening new faucets on the side; Q6 refuses to add treasury-key exposure for optics we can deliver with arithmetic. The sequencing matters as much as the numbers: drain first, measure continuously, and let nothing of real value touch MainNet until the custody ladder — not the marketing calendar — says the treasury can survive being wrong.
